    Plain-English guide

    Revenue — money earned from café sales before any costs.
    Food & beverage COGS — cost of the ingredients, coffee and packaging sold.
    Labour — wages, superannuation, leave accruals, plus the two staff seconded from NaNY.
    Overheads — running costs that aren't food or labour (fees, insurance, repairs, NaNY management fee).
    EBITDA — surplus before depreciation/amortisation; the cleanest view of trading performance.
    Net result — the bottom line: surplus or (deficit) after everything.
    FYTD — financial year to date (July through the focus month).
    AR / AP — money owed to us (receivables) / money we owe (payables).

    Labour cost by month and % of revenue

    Stacked bars: gross wages, NaNY secondment recharge?, superannuation and leave-accrual movements. Line: total labour as % of revenue. Labour is budgeted near 60% of revenue — well above the 30–35% commercial café benchmark, a structural feature of the supported-employment model. October and April spike because they contain five weekly pay runs.

    Penalty hours by month

    Saturday, Sunday, public-holiday and overtime hours are paid at a premium. This is the roster lever with the most leverage — moving trade out of the most expensive hours, or staffing them more thinly, changes the wage bill faster than anything else available.
